## The question

Where do NYC 311 “Damaged Tree” complaints concentrate across the five boroughs, how long do residents wait for resolution, and do the same hotspots hold up when verified across multiple years and adjusted for population?

## Verification and SQL

Before analyzing trends, results were cross-checked between the live **NYC Open Data API (SoQL)** for 2024 records and BigQuery’s public table (`bigquery-public-data.new_york_311`). Early zero-result errors were traced to two root causes: BigQuery’s public copy was **frozen on November 9, 2021**, and exact-string filters failed on spelling mismatches in complaint categories.

To rank the worst ZIP code per borough in a single pass, `queries.sql` uses a CTE paired with `ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY borough ORDER BY n DESC)` — separating top-per-group selection cleanly from raw aggregation and letting 2021 BigQuery results be compared side-by-side with 2024 SoQL extracts.

## What the requests show

### 1. Worst “Damaged Tree” ZIP code per borough (2024 vs. 2021)

Ranking 2024 “Damaged Tree” complaints by ZIP code within each borough surfaces clear geographic concentration — led by Brooklyn’s `11234` (**973** requests), Queens’s `11385` (**693**), and Staten Island’s `10312` (**674**).

### 2. Sticky hotspots across three years

Three of the five boroughs — Brooklyn (`11234`), Queens (`11385`), and Manhattan (`10031`) — recorded the exact same worst ZIP code in both **2021** and **2024**. That persistence points to underlying tree canopy and street-tree inventory rather than population density alone.

### 3. Speed follows damage already done

Across all 311 complaint types in 2024, **Staten Island** was the slowest borough overall with an average resolution time of **18.5 days**. Inside closed 2024 Staten Island tree cases, wait times diverged sharply by urgency: **“Tree Alive – in Poor Condition”** averaged **129.6 days** to close, while **“Entire Tree Has Fallen Down”** cleared in **36.1 days** — preventive reports waited more than three times as long as emergencies.

### 4. Seasonal volume, chronic queue

Damaged Tree volume on Staten Island is strongly seasonal, surging to **841** cases in **June** compared with roughly **110–210** cases per month during winter. Resolution times, however, never clear out: average wait time never drops below roughly **36 days** in any month and peaks at **91.2 days** in **September** as summer backlog carries forward.

### 5. Per-capita rates flip the ranking

Raw complaint counts make large, populous ZIP codes look worst. Ranked **per 1,000 residents** on Staten Island, **10308** moves into first place, followed by **10312** and then **10306** — confirming that raw totals largely reflect population size and local tree density rather than per-resident complaint intensity alone.
